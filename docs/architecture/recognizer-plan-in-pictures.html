Five changes to how dimensions are found, each shown as today and after

Green means found and trusted, blue means found, amber means a person should look, red means wrong. The drawings are simplified sketches, not real output.

1

See the whole dimension, not just its arrows

A dimension has five parts. Today we use two of them. Using all five tells us what is being measured.

2

Two finders, and a label that says whether they agree

One finder starts at the arrows, the other starts at the text. Where both arrive at the same dimension, we can trust it. This replaces the "80% confidence" line.

3

One callout, one balloon

Our biggest error today: a hole callout printed on two lines becomes four balloons. Text that sits together and shares one leader is one requirement.

4

A checklist of every dimension form

A published catalogue lists the forms a dimension can take. Marking each one handled or not tells us what is left, instead of finding out drawing by drawing. The marks below are my assessment today; the answer key will confirm them.

5

Angles measured from their two legs

Today an angle is judged from the direction of its two arrowheads, which is only good to about 3 degrees. Measuring between the two legs is direct, and the leg length picks the right row of the tolerance table.

Later, not now

A model that learns what each line is

Today I tell outline from dimension line by pen thickness, a rule that already needed tuning for one drawing. A small model can learn it from examples. It needs labelled drawings, which our own checked output can provide.
