What I found when I looked: our defaults are wrong on the heat sink today

The heat sink prints an ISO 2768 table and note 5 says "UNSPECIFIED TOLERANCE CLASS :- MEDIUM". That standard sets tolerance by size range. Our settings apply tolerance by decimal places. The limits on Form 3 differ.

Where a general tolerance can come from, and what we can do with each

readableA table printed as textHeat sink: range columns "OVER 30 UPTO 120", rows f, m, c, v. We already see every cell; today we rule them out as "general tolerance note". Parse the grid instead.
readableA standard named in a note"ISO 2768-mK", "TOLERANCE CLASS: MEDIUM". The standard's tables are fixed, so we ship them and only need the class letter.
readableDecimal-place block".X ±0.1 · .XX ±0.05 · ANGLES ±0.5°", common on ASME drawings. This is the only style our settings handle today.
ask onceA company standardThe bracket drawings say "REFER THINQX STANDARD FOR UNSPECIFIED TOLERANCE". Not on the sheet. Entered once as a customer profile, reused on every drawing from them.
needs OCRTitle block that is a pictureNo text to read. Options: read just that region with a small local OCR, or ask once and save to the customer profile. The intake card says which.
not thereNothing statedThe app asks, and marks every default-toleranced row "tolerance assumed" until someone confirms.

How it would look in the app

1
Tolerance source card on first open. "Found: ISO 2768 medium, from note 5 and the table at A11." Confirm once, or pick another source.
2
Each row says where its tolerance came from. "113.4 ±0.3 · ISO 2768-m, 30 to 120" instead of an unexplained ±.
3
Settings drawer gets a scheme choice. From drawing · ISO 2768 class · Decimal places · Custom table · Customer profile.
4
Title block fields prefill Part Info. Part number, description, material, revision, drawn by and date use the same reading. Form 1 starts filled.
